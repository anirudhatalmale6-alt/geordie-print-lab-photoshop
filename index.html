Ink colour

A screen prints one colour. On every setting of Screen from except the garment one, this changes the colour of the dots and nothing else. On the garment setting it is one of the two colours the dots are worked out from, so it does move them.

Adjustments

Brightness / contrast

Both of these are curves, not flat shifts. Brightness lifts the middle and leaves pure black and pure white where they are, so a strong colour gets brighter instead of going pale. Contrast steepens the middle the same way and never clips the ends.

Hue / saturation

Vibrance puts the lift where there is room for it - most into the muted colours, none into one that is already at full strength, none at all into grey, and it holds skin back so faces do not go orange. Reach for this one when a design looks flat.

Brightness by colour

The same six as Photoshop. Each one lightens or darkens just that family of colours and nothing else. Greys are left alone on purpose - a grey pixel has no colour to belong to.

One colour on its own

The six above sit 60 degrees apart, so a colour landing between two of them is shared between two sliders. That is right for orange and wrong for a lime green, which reads as roughly half green and half yellow and gets dragged about by the Yellows slider. Slide the top one to your colour and it comes out of the six entirely - one slider, one colour, and nothing else in the artwork moves. All the way left is off.
